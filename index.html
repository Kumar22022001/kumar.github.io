About

Greetings! I am a doctoral candidate at the Instituto de Astronomía y Ciencias Planetarias, Universidad de Atacama, Chile. I obtained my Bachelor's & Master's at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Bhopal, with my Master's research carried out at the Institute for Computational Cosmology, Durham University.

I work at the intersection of observational and data-driven astronomy, focusing on the formation and evolution of galaxies — particularly those whose histories are shaped by major/minor merger events. I enjoy building interpretable machine-learning tools that turn large survey datasets into physical insight, scalable to next-generation facilities like LSST and Euclid.

I am actively looking toward postdoctoral positions in observational studies of galaxies and black holes, and I'm always happy to discuss collaborations, galaxies, or the Atacama skies.

Research

My research sits at the intersection of extragalactic astronomy and data science: I use observational data, machine learning, and interpretable statistical models to understand how galaxies form, merge, and evolve across different environments.

Current PhD focus

At UDA I am studying how galaxy morphology changes from cluster cores to their outskirts, combining multiple characterization techniques — including machine-learning-based methods — to investigate how galaxy properties relate to their environment and what that tells us about galaxy evolution as a whole.

Selected Publication

The PAU Survey: Uncovering the connection between intrinsic and observed galaxy properties using symbolic regression Adarsh Kumar et al. — Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society (MNRAS), 2025. Read paper →

Master's thesis work carried out at Durham University with the GALFORM semi-analytical model and PAU Survey mock catalogs (out to z ~ 1.99). The derived expressions can be evaluated instantly for millions of galaxies with an accuracy comparable to traditional SED fitting codes, making them well-suited for upcoming surveys like LSST and Euclid.
